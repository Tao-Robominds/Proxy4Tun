\begin{abstract}
Tunnel-lining segmentation supports point-cloud inspection, but changing tunnel conditions and scarce reference labels complicate quality assessment. Proxy4Tun estimates segmentation accuracy from intermediate pipeline metrics, using one GT development subset and one expert-tuned setting per lining family. Across three Seg2Tunnel lining families, bounded sampling and uncertainty-guided Bayesian exploration produce 120 calibration runs. Model comparison and feature ablation identify a four-feature ridge-regression proxy that predicts mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) without requiring deployment labels. On 54 paired runs from 27 held-out subsets, the proxy achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.817 and a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.109; all 27 deliberately degraded runs are detected with no false alarms. For 22 cases selected by a proxy-score interval ([0.5, 0.7]), proxy-guided self-refinement increases mean mIoU from 0.738 to 0.759--0.768 across LLMs. Retrospective analysis shows that cases with intermediate initial GT mIoU gain 0.097 on average, versus 0.005 for the remaining cases. Proxy4Tun demonstrates that limited development supervision can be transferred to deployment quality control and self-refinement. The observed concentration of gains motivate further research into predicting self-refinement benefits without GT, rather than an optimal deployment selection rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Regular inspection of segmental tunnel linings supports structural health assessment and long-term operational safety. However, large tunnel networks and restricted access make manual inspection resource-intensive, motivating cost-effective data collection and analysis~\cite{attard2018,montero2015,strauss2020sensing}. Laser scanning can quickly capture the lining surface as a 3D point cloud~\cite{huang2021bim,sjolander2023towards,weidner2024generalized}. Many downstream inspection tasks require first assigning each point to its corresponding lining segment. In practice, segmentation accuracy can change with lining geometry, joint layout, occlusion, and scan quality. Manually checking each new output or fine-tuning its parameters imposes a recurring burden. Practitioners therefore need a way to assess whether an output is accurate enough to accept, needs self-refinement, or should be flagged for human review when new GT labels are unavailable (Fig.~\ref{fig:deployment_qc_gap}).

Regular inspection of segmental tunnel linings supports structural health assessment and operational safety, and laser scanning captures the lining surface as a 3D point cloud quickly enough to make network-scale inspection practical~\cite{attard2018,montero2015,strauss2020sensing,huang2021bim,sjolander2023towards,weidner2024generalized}. Most downstream inspection tasks first require assigning each point to its lining block. Segmentation accuracy, however, changes with lining geometry, joint layout, occlusion, and scan quality, and checking every new output against manual labels or re-tuning parameters by hand is a recurring cost. Practitioners need to decide whether an output is accurate enough to accept, should be refined, or must be flagged for human review, without new ground-truth (GT) labels (Fig.~\ref{fig:deployment_qc_gap}).

Existing segmentation approaches can be compared using two practical deployment questions: (i) how they cope with changing tunnel and scanning conditions, and (ii) what evidence they provide that a specific output is accurate (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual_positioning}). Engineer-designed geometric pipelines use hand-crafted rules and thresholds with interpretable checks, but changes in geometry or scan quality can require manual tuning or redesign~\cite{huang2021bim,weidner2024generalized,wang2025automated}. Supervised models can be retrained on labelled data and may provide confidence or uncertainty scores, but these scores must be checked against actual accuracy before they can support QC on new scans~\cite{su2024review,cha2024deep}. Foundation-model pipelines can reduce per-project annotation and support adaptation through prompts and preprocessing parameters~\cite{bommasani2021foundation,kirillov2023sam,ye2024sam}. However, the ability to adjust a pipeline does not establish whether the resulting segmentation is better or accurate enough to accept.

Previous studies show that segmentation accuracy can be estimated without GT labels for the new case. In medical imaging, for example, quality estimators learn from labelled development data or assess new outputs through labelled reference collections~\cite{valindria2017rca,robinson2018segmentationQuality,fournel2021segmentationQC}. It remains unclear how well these approaches transfer to tunnel point-cloud pipelines, where annotations are limited and processing depends on engineering reference configurations. This motivates our central research question: \emph{can measurements produced during tunnel segmentation be calibrated using a small labelled subset and an expert-tuned reference configuration per lining family, then used to estimate accuracy and guide parameter self-refinement on unseen scans without new GT labels?}

\begin{figure*}[t]

\label{fig:conceptual_positioning}

Proxy4Tun addresses this question by estimating the accuracy of a candidate segmentation from intermediate pipeline measurements. These measurements describe two aspects of the processing: preprocessing quality (PQ), which reflects the completeness and geometric plausibility of the representation supplied to the segmentation stage, and structure coherence (SC), which reflects whether the resulting label boundaries agree with detected joint evidence, together with segmentation coverage and consistency across rings. During development, we learn how these measurements relate to GT mean mIoU, then freeze the estimator for deployment. Our hypothesis is that parameter changes produce recognisable patterns in these measurements when processing succeeds or fails, and that their relationship with accuracy persists sufficiently on unseen scans from the same lining family.

Within a pipeline adapted from SAM4Tun~\cite{ye2025sam4tun}, we generate calibration runs using one labelled development subset and one expert-tuned reference configuration per lining family (staggered, continuous, and complex). We first sample settings across prescribed parameter ranges, then use uncertainty-guided Bayesian exploration to select further settings for evaluation. All runs reuse the same scan annotations. We test the resulting estimator on held-out scans and deliberately degraded outputs, and investigate whether its scores can help select useful LLM-proposed parameter changes in a limited self-refinement study. The approach is deployment-label-free: labels and expert references are required only during development, but neither are used during scoring or self-refinement. 
In a conceptual map of adaptability versus QC strength (Fig.~\ref{fig:conceptual_positioning}), we place Proxy4Tun in the high-QC, moderate-adaptability region, as it combines bounded parameter self-refinement with explicit, label-free accuracy estimation. 

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A deployment-label-free QC framework that learns to estimate tunnel-segmentation accuracy from PQ and SC measurements using limited offline annotations.
\item An empirical evaluation of which measurements transfer across lining families and whether the estimator distinguishes reference outputs from deliberately degraded counterparts.
\item A proof-of-concept (PoC) study testing whether proxy scores can guide the selection of bounded parameter adjustments proposed by a LLM.
\end{enumerate}

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:methods} presents the methodology and experimental design. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the results, Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses findings and limitations, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes.


\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The review follows four connected questions: how segmentation methods adapt to changing conditions, how their outputs can be assessed without new GT labels, how calibration data can be obtained with limited annotations, and how assessment feedback can guide self-refinement.

\subsection{Tunnel segmentation and adaptation}

Tunnel point-cloud segmentation methods differ in how they use geometric knowledge and labelled data, and how they accommodate changing conditions. Engineer-designed pipelines use geometric rules and thresholds to identify lining surfaces and segment boundaries, but their settings may need adjustment when tunnel geometry, joint layout, or scan quality changes~\cite{huang2021bim,weidner2024generalized,wang2025automated}. Supervised methods learn features from labelled point clouds: PointNet++ captures local geometry at multiple spatial scales~\cite{qi2017pointnet}, while RandLA-Net emphasises efficient processing of large point clouds~\cite{hu2020randla}. Instance-segmentation methods such as Mask3D and TD3D additionally distinguish individual objects within a scene~\cite{schult2023mask3d,kolodiazhnyi2024td3d}. Tunnel-specific methods can incorporate engineering knowledge into this learning process; LiningNet, for example, integrates structural geometry into supervised segmentation of tunnel linings~\cite{lin2025liningnet}. However, labelled tunnel data remain limited, and adapting supervised models to substantially different conditions may require additional annotations and retraining~\cite{su2024review, cha2024deep}.

Foundation-model pipelines reduce the need for task-specific training by using models pre-trained on large datasets. One practical approach is to project point clouds into two-dimensional images that can be processed by vision models. Prompt-based segmentation models include SAM~\cite{kirillov2023sam}, SAM2~\cite{ravi2024sam2}, and SEEM~\cite{zou2023segment}; infrastructure applications of such models include crack segmentation~\cite{ge2024cracksam,ye2024sam} and Scan-to-BIM workflows~\cite{wang2024omni,pan2024zero,ye2026maintenance}. For tunnel linings, SAM4Tun unfolds point clouds into panoramic images and generates template-based prompts for SAM to segment lining components without task-specific model training~\cite{ye2025sam4tun}. However, its preprocessing and prompting stages still depend on parameters that reflect assumptions about lining geometry and scanning conditions. Changes in those conditions can therefore require parameter adjustment even when the segmentation model itself remains fixed.

R4Tun addresses this parameter-adaptation problem by using reference information, intermediate pipeline outputs, and engineering knowledge to guide LLM-proposed adjustments to SAM4Tun within prescribed parameter ranges~\cite{taoR4Tun}. Its adaptation proceeds without GT access, while the resulting segmentation accuracy is evaluated against GT offline. This supports the use of structured context to propose adjustments, but leaves a separate assessment task: determining the accuracy of the initial and adjusted outputs when new GT labels are unavailable.

\subsection{Quality estimation without new GT labels}

Existing studies estimate segmentation quality from observable properties of the data and segmentation outputs. In medical imaging, Kohlberger et al. use segmentation features to predict overlap error and Dice scores~\cite{kohlberger2012evaluating}, while Robinson et al. learn to predict quality from image--segmentation pairs~\cite{robinson2018segmentationQuality}. For point clouds, LidarMetaSeg combines input features with network confidence measures to estimate the quality of connected semantic segments in driving scenes~\cite{colling2021lidarmetaseg}. Consistency between processing outputs can also provide evidence, although agreement does not guarantee correctness: different LiDAR processing streams can agree on an incorrect label~\cite{bogdoll2025labelfree}. These studies suggest that data properties and output consistency can help assess quality, provided their relationship with actual accuracy is established.

Reference-based approaches provide another source of assessment evidence. Reverse Classification Accuracy (RCA) treats a new segmentation as provisional labels, trains a reverse classifier, and evaluates that classifier on labelled reference images~\cite{valindria2017rca}. Its performance on those references is used to estimate the quality of the original segmentation. ConfIC-RCA further uses retrieval of relevant reference images and in-context reverse segmentation to estimate quality~\cite{cosarinsky2026conficrca}. These methods avoid requiring GT for the new case, but still use labelled reference cases during assessment.

Together, these approaches motivate combining measurements of processing quality and output consistency with reference information. Proxy4Tun investigates this idea using PQ and SC measurements. \hl{Expert configurations guide offline exploration, but the final scoring features no longer require family-reference information.} The next question is how to learn the relationship between these measurements and accuracy when only limited scans are labelled.

\subsection{Calibration with limited labelled data}

Training a quality estimator requires examples spanning successful and degraded segmentations, but these examples need not require additional scan annotations. Robinson et al. generate multiple segmentations by varying model settings and reuse existing annotations to obtain quality targets~\cite{robinson2018segmentationQuality}. The same labelled input can therefore provide several examples of how processing choices affect accuracy. The challenge is to select useful settings without evaluating every possible combination.

Bayesian optimisation provides ways to use expert knowledge and previous evaluations to guide this selection. BOPrO incorporates user beliefs about promising parameter regions, while $\pi$BO incorporates a probability distribution describing where the optimum is expected to lie~\cite{souza2020prior,hvarfner2022pibo}. These studies support using prior knowledge to inform where evaluations are performed. Space-filling sampling serves a complementary purpose by spreading initial settings across the chosen parameter ranges~\cite{joe2008constructing}. After these settings have been evaluated, a Gaussian-process model can identify where its predictions remain uncertain and guide further evaluations~\cite{gramacy2009adaptive,gramacy2020surrogates}.

Proxy4Tun combines these ideas to collect learning examples around an expert reference. The reference and prescribed bounds define which parameter variations are explored; initial space-filling sampling and then subsequent uncertainty-guided evaluations seek a range of processing outcomes. Each output is assessed using the same existing scan annotations. Here, the aim is to learn from both successful and deliberately degraded segmentations, rather than concentrate the search only on high-accuracy settings.

\subsection{Feedback-guided self-refinement}

LLM-based agents can revise results by proposing changes, evaluating their effects, and using feedback to guide subsequent attempts. Self-Refine asks the model to critique and revise its output, while Reflexion records lessons from task feedback for later attempts~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine,shinn2023reflexion}. AlphaEvolve and the Darwin G\"odel Machine extend it to modifying programs or agent code using automated evaluation~\cite{novikov2025alphaevolve,zhang2025darwin}. However, repeated revision does not guarantee improvement. Self-correction without external feedback can fail, and increasing an imperfect proxy score can reduce performance when assessed separately~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect,gao2023overoptimization}.

Feedback is more useful for proposing changes when it also describes what may be wrong. ReAct demonstrates how observations from the environment can inform subsequent actions~\cite{yao2023react}. \hl{In Proxy4Tun, PQ and SC measurements provide this context: an incomplete depth map or disagreement between detected joints and predicted boundaries can
indicate which stages warrant inspection.} A compact linear proxy makes each measurement's contribution to the predicted score explicit, following the rationale for interpretable models~\cite{rudin2019stop}. The measurements support diagnosis, while the score provides a common basis for comparing candidate outputs.

We test this combination through a feasibility study on cases whose initial proxy score falls in a deployment-relevant interval ($[0.5,0.7]$). The LLM proposes parameter changes within prescribed bounds, the pipeline produces new segmentations, and proxy feedback guides which candidates are retained. The study asks whether this loop can improve segmentation without GT access during self-refinement. Longer-term self-improvement and changes to the agent itself remain outside its scope.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methods}

Proxy4Tun has two phases. During development, we vary pipeline parameters on labelled scans and learn how PQ and SC measurements relate to segmentation accuracy. At deployment, the frozen proxy estimates mIoU without new GT labels. An LLM uses the measurements and engineering context to propose parameter changes within a fixed budget (three candidate runs per case), and the proxy selects the candidate with the highest score. We first define the task and available information, then describe calibration, self-refinement, and experimental evaluation.

\subsection{Task definition}
\label{sec:problem-def}

Given a raw point cloud of a tunnel section, the task is to assign each point a structural label: background, key segment (K), adjacent segments (B), and standard segments (A). Seg2Tunnel, the benchmark dataset we use, groups linings into three families (staggered, continuous, and complex). These families differ in ring geometry and acquisition conditions and, most critically for our task, in the key-block (K) layout: ring-to-ring repeating, fixed ring-wise order, and no repeating pattern (Fig.~\ref{fig:task}). 

Proxy4Tun assesses a completed segmentation run. Given its intermediate processing outputs, the proxy estimates the mIoU that would otherwise require comparison with GT labels. The score supports assessment of the current result and comparison with outputs produced by alternative parameter settings. At deployment, the proxy remains fixed and scoring uses the new run's intermediate outputs. GT labels for the new scan are unavailable to both the proxy scoring and the self-refinement process.

\subsection{Framework overview}
\label{sec:method}

The framework connects three components: a parameterised segmentation pipeline, an offline procedure for learning the mIoU proxy, and a deployment-time self-refinement loop. The pipeline produces both segmentations and the measurements used to assess them. Calibration learns the relationship between these measurements and GT mIoU. self-refinement uses the frozen proxy to compare new candidates.

The processing algorithms remain fixed throughout the study. Exploration and self-refinement change only permitted parameter values.


\subsubsection{Segmentation pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The pipeline is adapted from SAM4Tun~\cite{ye2025sam4tun} for the three Seg2Tunnel lining families~\cite{lin2024seg2tunnel}. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} illustrates the processing stages.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figs/pipeline.pdf}
\caption{Segmentation pipeline adapted from SAM4Tun. Geometric alignment, filtering, and enhancement produce a depth representation for boundary detection and template-based SAM prompting. The resulting masks are reprojected onto the point cloud. Intermediate outputs provide PQ and SC measurements. The stage algorithms remain fixed; exploration and self-refinement adjust their parameters.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Unfolding.}
RANSAC-based cross-sectional ellipse fitting and centreline estimation establish a cylindrical coordinate system~\cite{fischler1981ransac}. The lining surface is represented panoramically, with depth describing radial distance from the centreline. Coordinate orientation is fixed so that block positions retain the same meaning across runs.

\item \textbf{Denoising.}
Radial masks and grid-based point-density filtering remove points inconsistent with the expected lining surface. The filtering follows the density-based principle underlying DBSCAN~\cite{ester1996dbscan}. Its settings affect both unwanted-point removal and the amount of lining data retained.

\item \textbf{Enhancement.}
Curvature-guided interpolation and progressive upsampling improve the completeness of the depth representation before detection, while preserving joint boundaries.

\item \textbf{Boundary detection.}
Orientation-specific Hough transforms identify candidate ring and block boundaries~\cite{duda1972hough}. These detections are organised according to the expected family layout.

\item \textbf{Segmentation and reprojection.}
The detected layout defines template-based prompts for SAM~\cite{kirillov2023sam}. The resulting image masks are mapped back to the point cloud to obtain three-dimensional block labels.
\end{enumerate}

The \emph{family reference configuration} (\emph{family reference}) specifies a complete parameter configuration. A candidate update lists only the parameters to change and is applied to an explicitly recorded base configuration. Unchanged parameters retain their base values. Appendix~\ref{app:anchor-parameter} lists the expert family references, and Appendix~\ref{app:search-bounds} provides the exploration bounds.


\subsubsection{Generating calibration examples}
\label{sec:bo-experience}

Calibration requires examples linking observable pipeline measurements
to segmentation accuracy under successful and degraded parameter
settings. We generate these examples by varying pipeline parameters
on one labelled development subset per lining family. All runs on
that subset are evaluated against the same annotations, so additional
runs require computation but no additional scan labelling.

We collect the examples in two stages. First, scrambled-Sobol sampling
provides 32 valid trials spread across the prescribed parameter
ranges~\cite{joe2008constructing}. Continuous parameters are scaled
to their bounds, while discrete parameters are mapped to their
permitted values. This provides broad initial coverage. Second, Bayesian exploration adds eight valid trials at settings
whose effects on accuracy remain uncertain. A Gaussian-process
(GP) response model learns from the completed evaluations and
estimates both GT mIoU and its predictive uncertainty at candidate
settings. Selecting settings with high uncertainty directs the
remaining budget toward gaps in the initial
evidence~\cite{gramacy2009adaptive,gramacy2020surrogates}. The GP uses a Mat\'ern kernel with $\nu=2.5$, a white-noise term,
and normalised targets. At each iteration, we draw a random pool
of 2048 candidate settings within the parameter bounds and evaluate
the candidate with the largest predictive standard deviation.
The GP is updated after each additional valid evaluation. This
selection explores uncertainty in the accuracy response; it does
not directly maximise segmentation accuracy or minimise the final
proxy's prediction error.

At the end, each family contributes 40 valid runs, giving 120 runs across the
three families. Each
valid run records its parameter setting, PQ and SC measurements,
and GT mIoU. These records serve two distinct purposes: the GP
uses parameter settings and GT mIoU to guide further exploration,
while the final accuracy proxy uses PQ and SC measurements to
estimate GT mIoU.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:bo_exploration} summarises the procedure, and 
Appendices~\ref{app:bo-config} and~\ref{app:search-bounds}
provide the sampling configuration and parameter bounds.
Figure~\ref{fig:b-p} connects this example-generation procedure
to the subsequent feature comparison.

\caption{Calibration-example generation and feature comparison.
(a) Each lining family contributes 32 valid scrambled-Sobol trials

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Generating calibration examples through space-filling
sampling and Bayesian exploration}
\label{alg:bo_exploration}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Development subset $D_f$ with GT annotations $Y_f$
\Require \hl{Reference configuration $P_f^{\mathrm{ref}}$; expected ring count $N_{\mathrm{exp}}=10$}
\Require Parameter bounds $\Theta_f$;
         valid-trial budgets $N_0=32$, $N_1=8$;
         candidate-pool size $M=2048$
\Ensure Calibration records $\mathcal{T}_f$
\State $\mathcal{T}_f\gets\emptyset$;
       $n_0\gets0$; $n_1\gets0$
\State Initialise a scrambled-Sobol sequence
\Statex \textit{Stage 1: broad initial coverage}
\While{$n_0<N_0$}
    \State Draw the next Sobol point and map it to
           permitted parameter values $\theta\in\Theta_f$
    \State $P\gets\operatorname{Apply}
           (P_f^{\mathrm{ref}},\theta)$
    \State $R\gets\operatorname{Pipeline}(D_f,P)$
    \If{$R$ is valid and required measurements are available}
        \State \hl{$\mathbf{x}\gets\operatorname{Features}(R,N_{\mathrm{exp}})$}
        \State $y\gets\operatorname{mIoU}(R,Y_f)$
        \State Append $(\theta,\mathbf{x},y)$
               to $\mathcal{T}_f$
        \State $n_0\gets n_0+1$
    \Else
        \State Log the failed or incomplete trial
    \EndIf
\EndWhile
\Statex \textit{Stage 2: Bayesian exploration}
\State Fit GP to the $(\theta,y)$ pairs in $\mathcal{T}_f$
\While{$n_1<N_1$}
    \State Draw $M$ random candidate settings
           $\mathcal{P}_t$ within $\Theta_f$
    \State $\theta\gets
           \arg\max_{\vartheta\in\mathcal{P}_t}
           \sigma_t(\vartheta)$
    \State $P\gets\operatorname{Apply}
           (P_f^{\mathrm{ref}},\theta)$
    \State $R\gets\operatorname{Pipeline}(D_f,P)$
    \If{$R$ is valid and required measurements are available}
        \State \hl{$\mathbf{x}\gets\operatorname{Features}(R,N_{\mathrm{exp}})$}
        \State $y\gets\operatorname{mIoU}(R,Y_f)$
        \State Append $(\theta,\mathbf{x},y)$
               to $\mathcal{T}_f$
        \State Update GP using the expanded $\mathcal{T}_f$
        \State $n_1\gets n_1+1$
    \Else
        \State Log the failed or incomplete trial
    \EndIf
\EndWhile
\State \Return $\mathcal{T}_f$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{PQ and SC measurements}
\label{sec:features}

For each completed run, we compute ten candidate measurements without using any ground-truth (GT) labels (Table~\ref{tab:candidate-features}). The measurements fall into two groups: preprocessing quality (PQ) and structure coherence (SC).

PQ captures depth-map completeness, point support, geometric alignment, and ring-count plausibility. The ring-count measurement compares the detected number of rings to an expected count $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$ (set to 10 in our experiments), which is typically available from design or acquisition metadata. Using $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$ makes the proxy less sensitive to ambiguous ring evidence.

SC captures internal agreement between pipeline stages: alignment between detected joint evidence and predicted label boundaries, segmentation coverage over the valid-depth region, and consistency of the label ordering across rings. SC is particularly useful for the complex lining family because it does not assume a fixed keystone pattern. However, high SC does not guarantee correctness: the joint detections and the segmentation can share correlated errors (e.g., when joint detections also guide SAM prompting).

\subsubsection{Learning the accuracy proxy}
\label{sec:ridge-proxy}

The proxy maps PQ and SC measurements to an estimated mIoU. We use Ridge regression to obtain a compact model with an explicit scoring rule. Regularisation stabilises estimation under correlated predictors~\cite{hoerl1970ridge,pedregosa2011sklearn}, and the linear form makes each measurement's contribution directly interpretable.

For a feature vector $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, the prediction is
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}(\mathbf{x})
=
b+\sum_{j=1}^{d}
w_j\frac{x_j-\mu_j}{s_j},
\label{eq:ridge-proxy}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_j$ and $s_j$ are the mean and standard deviation estimated on the training data, $w_j$ is the fitted coefficient, and $b$ is the intercept. The coefficients minimise
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{train}}}
\left(y_i-b-\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_i\right)^2
+\alpha\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2,
\label{eq:ridge-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_i$ is the standardised feature vector and $\alpha$ controls regularisation.

We compare PQ-only, SC-only, combined, and compact combined models (Table~\ref{tab:proxy-ablation}). The notation $P(q)$ denotes the PQ-only model, $P(c)$ denotes the SC-only model, and $P(q{+}c)$ denotes their combination.

Feature-removal analysis examines the contribution of individual measurements. Leave-one-family-out (LOFO) validation assesses transfer: models are fitted using calibration runs from two families and evaluated on the third. Feature selection and regularisation tuning are performed using only the training data within each fold, without access to the held-out family's labels.

The compact feature set is chosen using development-time comparisons of prediction, transfer, and feature coverage. Its purpose is to retain PQ and SC information in a small model, rather than to maximise every development metric. The final pooled model is then fitted on all 120 calibration runs and frozen.

The proxy score provides a common basis for comparing outputs. Regression coefficients describe fitted associations with accuracy; they are not causal prescriptions for parameter changes. We therefore evaluate the compact proxy as a practical scoring rule, without claiming superiority over nonlinear alternatives.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:proxy} separates model development from deployment scoring.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{\hl{Proxy calibration and deployment scoring}}
\label{alg:proxy}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require \hl{Calibration records $\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_f\mathcal{T}_f$; candidate feature sets}
\Require \hl{Fixed extraction conventions, including $N_{\mathrm{exp}}=10$}
\Ensure \hl{Frozen proxy package $g$}
\For{\hl{each candidate feature set $V$}}
    \For{\hl{each held-out family $f$}}
        \State \hl{Use the other two families as training data}
        \State \hl{Fit feature scaling and select $\alpha$ within the training data}
        \State \hl{Fit the model and predict the held-out family's mIoU}
    \EndFor
    \State \hl{Record training and LOFO prediction metrics}
\EndFor
\State \hl{Select the compact set by LOFO Spearman, retaining PQ and SC}
\State \hl{Fit pooled scaling and Ridge regression on all 120 calibration records}
\State \hl{Freeze feature definitions, order, scaler, coefficients, and intercept}
\Statex
\State \hl{Receive a new completed run $r$}
\State \hl{$\mathbf{x}\gets\operatorname{Features}(r,N_{\mathrm{exp}})$ in the frozen feature order}
\State \hl{$\hat{y}\gets g(\mathbf{x})$}
\State \Return \hl{$\hat{y}$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Proxy-guided parameter self-refinement}
\label{sec:agent-design}

The proxy estimates accuracy but does not determine which parameters should change. An LLM agent supplies this proposal step (Fig.~\ref{fig:agent}). Its context includes the family reference configurations, current settings, intermediate outputs, PQ and SC measurements, proxy scores, and engineering knowledge linking observable problems to possible adjustments.

We implement the agent's refinement process as an explicit, inspect/compare/update loop by chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting~\cite{wei2023cot,yao2023react}: the agent first grounds itself in the current evidence, then links observed symptoms to failure modes, and finally proposes a small, bounded edit that can be verified by re-running the pipeline. In our implementation, the agent returns (i) a parameter adjustment constrained to pre-defined ranges and (ii) a brief human-readable rationale summarising the evidence and intended effect. Prompt structure and knowledge excerpts are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:ontology} and Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figs/agent.pdf}
\caption{LLM-guided parameter proposals. The agent compares the current run with engineering references, inspects intermediate outputs and PQ/SC measurements, and returns a bounded parameter update with a rationale. After execution, the frozen proxy scores the candidate. Candidate retention depends only on the proxy score.}
\label{fig:agent}
\end{figure*}

Each self-refinement round has three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inspect and propose.}
The agent (a) compares current intermediate outputs and PQ/SC measurements to the reference configuration, (b) identifies a likely failure mode using the engineering knowledge, and (c) returns a structured update: \emph{symptom} $\rightarrow$ \emph{evidence} $\rightarrow$ \emph{bounded parameter diff} $\rightarrow$ \emph{rationale}.

\item \textbf{Validate and execute.}
The update is checked for valid parameter names, permitted values, and fixed pipeline conventions. A valid candidate is executed and its PQ and SC measurements are calculated.

\item \textbf{Score and record.}
The frozen proxy estimates candidate accuracy. The configuration, base settings, proposed changes, execution status, measurements, and score are recorded for subsequent feedback and final selection.
\end{enumerate}

Each selected subset receives three self-refinement rounds without early stopping. The starting configuration remains eligible throughout. Failed or unscorable attempts are recorded and excluded from selection.

The retained result is the successfully scored candidate with the highest proxy score, including the starting configuration:
\begin{equation}
r^\star
=
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{r\in\{0\}\cup\mathcal{V}}
\hat{y}_r,
\qquad
P^\star=P_{r^\star},
\label{eq:self-refinement-selection}
\end{equation}
where $r=0$ denotes the Bayesian-exploration baseline and $\mathcal{V}$ contains the successfully scored self-refinement rounds.

SC measurements contribute to the proxy and inform the agent's diagnosis. They do not impose a separate retention gate. Thus, candidate selection is determined solely by the frozen proxy score, without GT access.

The final score assigns the retained output to a study operating band:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{QC}(\hat{y})=
\begin{cases}
\text{flag}, & \hat{y}<0.5,\\
\text{refine}, & 0.5\leq\hat{y}\leq0.7,\\
\text{accept}, & \hat{y}>0.7.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:qc-bands}
\end{equation}
These bands illustrate assessment decisions; they are not validated engineering acceptance criteria. A result may remain in the self-refinement band after the fixed budget is exhausted. Crossing 0.7 during an intermediate round does not stop the experiment.

The LLM, proxy, and processing algorithms remain fixed across rounds. Only permitted pipeline parameters change. Algorithm~\ref{alg:reflective_adaptation} summarises the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Proxy-guided parameter self-refinement}
\label{alg:reflective_adaptation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require New subset $D$; Bayesian-exploration baseline $P_0$; expected ring count $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$
\Require Engineering knowledge, parameter bounds, frozen proxy package $g$
\Require self-refinement budget $R=3$
\Ensure Retained configuration $P^\star$ and QC status
\State Execute the starting configuration $P_0$
\State Extract measurements and compute $\hat{y}_0$
\State $P^\star\gets P_0$; $\hat{y}^\star\gets\hat{y}_0$
\State Initialise history with the starting run
\For{$r=1,\ldots,R$}
    \State Assemble context from settings, outputs, measurements, scores, and history
    \State Obtain a bounded parameter overlay from the LLM (applied to the fixed Bayesian-exploration baseline $P_0$)
    \State Record base configuration $P_0$ and validate the overlay
    \If{proposal invalid}
        \State Record failure; \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \State Execute candidate configuration $P_r$
    \If{execution or feature extraction fails}
        \State Record failure; \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \State Extract $\mathbf{x}_r\gets\operatorname{Features}(P_r,N_{\mathrm{exp}})$; compute $\hat{y}_r\gets g(\mathbf{x}_r)$
    \State Add candidate outputs and score to history
    \If{$\hat{y}_r>\hat{y}^\star$}
        \State $P^\star\gets P_r$; $\hat{y}^\star\gets\hat{y}_r$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$\hat{y}^\star<0.5$}
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets\mathrm{flag}$
\ElsIf{$\hat{y}^\star\leq0.7$}
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets\mathrm{refine}$
\Else
    \State $\mathrm{status}\gets\mathrm{accept}$
\EndIf
\State \Return $P^\star$, $\hat{y}^\star$, $\mathrm{status}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

The evaluation addresses three questions: whether PQ and SC support accuracy estimation, whether the frozen proxy distinguishes reference outputs from controlled degradations on unseen scans, and whether highest-score selection produces useful parameter self-refinement.


\subsubsection{Dataset and information separation}
\label{sec:dataset}

We use Seg2Tunnel subsets spanning staggered, continuous, and complex lining families~\cite{lin2024seg2tunnel}. The development subsets are 2-1, 3-1, and 5-1, respectively. Each supplies 40 calibration runs, giving 120 runs from three labelled development subsets.

Held-out evaluation uses 27 other subsets, with nine per family (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). These subsets are unseen during proxy fitting but come from the represented families and tunnel collection. The evaluation therefore examines transfer to unseen scans within this setting, rather than unrestricted transfer to new projects or lining geometries.

Parameter variation increases the number of observed processing outcomes without increasing the number of independently annotated scans. Development labels are used for calibration, model comparisons, and development-time degradation selection. Held-out GT is used for offline evaluation and retrospective self-refinement-panel construction only.

\subsubsection{Feature and model comparisons}
\label{sec:comparators}

The comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:proxy-ablation} use the same calibration trials and segmentation pipeline. We report training fit and LOFO performance separately. LOFO comparisons inform model development; evaluation on the 27 unseen subsets then tests the frozen proxy.

We compare the pooled compact proxy with three family-specific proxies using the same five features. The pooled model uses all 120 calibration runs, while each family-specific model uses its family's 40 runs. Model fitting does not use held-out labels.

A permutation control examines whether the features carry information about accuracy beyond differences between families. GT mIoU values are shuffled among calibration runs within each family, and the compact Ridge fitting procedure is repeated for 20 permutations. The real-label and shuffled-label results are compared to assess feature--accuracy association. This control does not itself establish the absence of leakage; GT isolation and fold-specific fitting are enforced separately.


\subsubsection{Paired degradation test}
\label{sec:stress-test}

Each held-out subset provides an expert-anchor reference output and a deliberately degraded counterpart, giving 54 scored runs. The degradation procedures use parameter overlays and boundary perturbations. Their construction is fixed using development information before held-out evaluation.

Both outputs in each pair are assessed using the same frozen feature definitions and proxy. Feature extraction uses the corresponding processing outputs and the same fixed extraction conventions (including $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$). GT mIoU is calculated separately to measure actual segmentation accuracy.

The test examines whether the proxy ranks the reference above its degraded counterpart and whether the fixed low-quality threshold detects the constructed degradation. These are controlled failure cases, not a sample of naturally occurring deployment failures. Consequently, the panel supports conclusions about discrimination under the tested perturbations, rather than general operational acceptance reliability.


\subsubsection{self-refinement feasibility panel}
\label{sec:poc-protocol}

The self-refinement experiment uses nine retrospectively selected cases. Selection is based on the Bayesian-exploration baseline run:
\begin{equation}
0.5\leq\hat{y}_0\leq0.7
\quad\text{and}\quad
0.5\leq y_0\leq0.7,
\label{eq:poc-selection}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}_0$ is the proxy estimate and $y_0$ is offline GT mIoU.

The proxy condition identifies outputs within the self-refinement band. The GT condition confirms that the selected outputs are below the study accuracy threshold. This second condition is used solely for offline panel construction; GT does not enter the self-refinement loop or candidate selection.

Bayesian exploration is performed only during development, using GT on the development subsets to evaluate candidate configurations and identify a strong per-family baseline. For each selected case, this Bayesian-exploration baseline configuration supplies the start. Three LLM-proposed candidates are attempted, and the highest-scoring valid output is retained together with the option of keeping the start. Offline GT evaluation then compares the starting and retained segmentations.

\subsubsection{Evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

Offline segmentation accuracy is measured by mIoU. For evaluated block class $c$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{IoU}_c
=
\frac{\operatorname{TP}_c}
{\operatorname{TP}_c+\operatorname{FP}_c+\operatorname{FN}_c},
\qquad
\operatorname{mIoU}
=
\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\operatorname{IoU}_c,
\end{equation}
where $C=6$ for staggered and continuous rings and $C=7$ for complex rings. The distinction concerns individual blocks rather than the broader K/B/A block types.

Proxy prediction error is measured by mean absolute error:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MAE}
=
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
|\hat{y}_i-y_i|,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}_i$ and $y_i$ are predicted and GT mIoU. MAE measures numerical prediction error. Spearman correlation $\rho$ measures whether the proxy orders runs consistently with GT accuracy. We report bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals for the correlation.

For the paired degradation test, ranking accuracy is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{PairRank}
=
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{s=1}^{M}
\mathbb{I}
\left[
\hat{y}_{s,\mathrm{ref}}>
\hat{y}_{s,\mathrm{deg}}
\right],
\end{equation}
where $M=27$. Tied scores do not count as successful rankings. Alarm precision and recall are calculated at the fixed threshold of 0.5 against the reference/degraded construction labels and interpreted within this controlled test.

For the pooled versus family-specific comparison, absolute prediction errors are aggregated within each subset, preserving the reference/degraded pairing. A two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test compares the resulting 27 paired subset-level errors.

For self-refinement, we report starting and selected proxy scores and GT mIoU for each case, including cases that retain the start. Mean changes summarise the panel, while per-case results show whether selection improves or worsens actual accuracy. Any $p$-values reported for self-refinement are exploratory and conditional on this retrospectively selected, nine-case panel: we use a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the nine paired mIoU differences (baseline versus retained). When summarising the effect on the full $n{=}27$ holdout panel, the remaining 18 unrefined subsets contribute zero change by construction; under the usual Wilcoxon convention these zeros are discarded, yielding the same test as on the nine attempted cases. We report this only as a descriptive check, not as a confirmatory significance claim.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This study supports a mechanism-level claim: a frozen, five-feature linear model over observable intrinsic metrics can serve as a deployment-label-free control signal for a tunnel segmentation pipeline. One labelled anchor per lining family is used during design-time calibration on Bayesian-exploration trials. At deployment, the frozen proxy estimates mIoU from intermediate pipeline artifacts using the same fixed extraction conventions (including $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$); family references may still be provided to the proposal agent (and to choose $P_0$), but are not inputs to the frozen scorer. Its scores support ranking and accept/refine/flag decisions using the study operating thresholds $\tau_{\mathrm{alarm}}=0.5$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{accept}}=0.7$, and selection between self-refinement rounds; the linear coefficients keep this control signal auditable.

\subsection{Key findings}

Three findings follow. First, SC alone provides the strongest reported transfer performance under LOFO (Table~\ref{tab:ablation-results}). Pruning the combined PQ{+}SC set from ten features to five improves transfer relative to the full combined set (LOFO Spearman 0.614$\rightarrow$0.678), with only a negligible increase in training MAE (0.096$\rightarrow$0.098). We still retain PQ features in the deployed lean proxy to preserve robustness and diagnostic coverage beyond SC-only, not because the combined set outperforms SC-only on LOFO.
Second, the unified proxy provides the best deployment
balance. Family-specific models raise absolute MAE (0.096 versus 0.086) and
reduce alarm precision on continuous and complex holdouts, while also requiring
separate calibrations.
Third, the proxy makes self-refinement auditable by ranking bounded LLM-proposed overlays using an explicit maximum-proxy rule: retain the highest proxy score among the Bayesian-exploration baseline and successfully scored self-refinement rounds (ties retain the earlier configuration).

Across the nine-case PoC study (27 round-to-round transitions; Table~\ref{tab:poc-selection-rounds}), the proxy score increases in 15 transitions. When the proxy increases, offline mIoU decreases in 3 transitions. This opposite-direction behaviour highlights that the proxy is an imperfect surrogate and should be used for bounded, auditable control decisions rather than treated as a GT mIoU replacement.

In practice, each pipeline release requires one expert-tuned anchor per
lining family and one offline calibration. Bounded Bayesian exploration turns
these anchors into labelled experience across healthy and degraded
configurations; feature ablation then freezes a unified proxy package
(feature definitions, feature-scaling statistics, coefficients, and operating thresholds, together with the fixed extraction conventions such as $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$). The deployed
proxy provides each new run with a deployment-label-free mIoU estimate and an accept/refine/flag decision from that package. In the retrospective PoC panel, proxy-guided self-refinement improved seven of nine subsets without deployment label access inside the loop. This calibration-and-self-refinement pattern can extend to other parameterised infrastructure pipelines with observable intermediate artifacts.

\subsection{Comparison with state-of-the-art}\label{sec:positioning}

Comparisons here are about operating points, not accuracy leaderboards. Supervised 3D models on Seg2Tunnel report strong in-distribution accuracy~\cite{lin2024seg2tunnel} but require per-point labels and GPU re-training for each new lining family, and provide no deployment-time accept/refine/flag gate. SAM4Tun reaches high accuracy on curated, per-case-tuned rings~\cite{ye2025sam4tun}, but a single fixed configuration transfers poorly off the reference and the pipeline emits no signal when it degrades. Relative to that static pipeline, Proxy4Tun adds an explicit accept/refine/flag gate without deployment labels. Relative to one-shot LLM parameter adapters, it adds a calibrated numeric proxy, so self-refinement can be \emph{assessed and selected} by an explicit estimated-quality signal rather than merely proposed.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

Three limitations define the current scope. First, calibration relies on one
expert-tuned anchor per lining family. Transfer therefore depends on how
closely a new tunnel matches its anchor in geometry, lining layout, point
density, and scanning conditions. Larger within-family variation may require
multiple anchors or additional exploration before the proxy is frozen.

Second, the deployed proxy is a compact five-feature linear Ridge model. This
choice provides transparency, auditability, and low deployment cost, but cannot
fully represent nonlinear interactions among pipeline stages or unfamiliar
failure patterns. More expressive nonlinear models, such as neural networks,
could improve performance in these settings, but would require a larger,
more diverse calibration dataset and would reduce interpretability.

Third, the reported alarm result is a paired \emph{reference-sibling baseline} / \emph{constructed degraded} stress test, and the nine-case self-refinement study is a retrospective, GT-filtered feasibility panel. Neither experiment estimates deployment false-alarm rates on naturally degraded runs, nor does the PoC simulate a deployment-style case-selection gate that admits refine-band cases by the proxy alone without the offline GT filter.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Proxy4Tun calibrates a deployment-label-free quality proxy for segmental tunnel-lining segmentation from design-time Bayesian exploration and deploys it as an auditable control signal. Exploration around one expert-tuned family reference per lining family produces 120 labelled trials spanning healthy to degraded configurations; feature ablation then distils evidence and coherence metrics into a frozen five-feature Ridge proxy with \emph{study operating thresholds} $\tau_{\mathrm{alarm}}=0.5$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{accept}}=0.7$ used to illustrate accept/refine/flag decisions (not validated engineering acceptance criteria). At deployment, the proxy estimates mIoU from a frozen package of intermediate artifacts and the expected ring count $N_{\mathrm{exp}}$ (ten in our experiments); it does not require family-reference statistics.

\begin{itemize}
\item On a paired stress test of 54 \emph{reference-sibling baseline} and \emph{constructed degraded} holdout runs, the frozen proxy achieves Spearman 0.827 and MAE 0.086, ranks the baseline run above its degraded sibling on every subset, and flags all 27 constructed degraded runs with no false alarms on the paired baseline runs. This is not a deployment false-alarm estimate on naturally occurring failures.
\item Coherence provides the strongest standalone signal, while leave-one-out pruning of the combined feature set improves the LOFO coefficient-transfer diagnostic relative to the full ten-feature set (LOFO Spearman 0.678 vs.\ 0.614). The unified proxy retains perfect alarm precision and recall under a single calibration and is therefore the deployment model.
\item On the 27-subset panel, mean mIoU rises from 0.456 (notebook expert configuration) to 0.722 (Bayesian-exploration baseline) to 0.757 after proxy-guided self-refinement of nine GT-filtered candidates. On the refined nine-case subset, mean mIoU increases from 0.569 to 0.675 ($+0.106667\approx{+}0.107$); when averaged over all 27 subsets this contributes $0.106667\times 9/27=0.035556\approx{+}0.036$ to the full-panel mean (0.722$\rightarrow$0.757).
\end{itemize}

The current scope is limited by single-anchor calibration and the compact linear model; greater within-family variation or more complex failure modes require broader calibration and potentially nonlinear proxies. Future work should evaluate multi-anchor calibration, uncertainty-aware abstention, larger self-refinement campaigns selected by the proxy gate alone, and external or naturally degraded alarm tests. These results show that intrinsic, stage-observable metrics can provide an auditable control plane for tunnel-segmentation pipelines when GT is unavailable.

\section{Tunnel ontology structure}\label{app:ontology}

The reflective agent consults a staged SAM4Tun ontology that encodes pipeline
artifacts, per-stage parameters and quality metrics, failure modes, diagnostic
rules, remediation actions, and inter-stage dependencies. The top-level schema
is summarised below:
We omit the complete YAML and show only the structure plus one representative
stage example.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Ontology top-level keys and stage inventory.},label={lst:ontology-structure}]
meta                  # version, domain, coordinate systems
artifacts             # data objects (cloud, depth map, masks, ...)
stages[]              # five pipeline stages:
  stage_1_centreline
  stage_2_denoise
  stage_3_upsample
  stage_4_detection
  stage_5_segmentation
inter_stage_dependencies[]   # upstream cause -> defer local retune
diagnostic_rules[]           # metric patterns -> failure modes
remediation_actions[]        # bounded parameter changes
agent_interface              # {state, diagnosis, proposal}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Example ontology stage: radial denoising failure modes.},label={lst:ontology-example}]
- id: stage_2_denoise
  name: Local density-difference-based denoising
  quality_metrics:
    - id: m_retained_ratio
      healthy: "lining kept; background removed"
    - id: m_bg_leakage
      healthy: "near zero"
  failure_modes:
    - id: f_over_gate
      name: Radial gate too tight
      symptom: "lining removed; holes in later depth map"
    - id: f_under_gate
      name: Radial gate too loose
      symptom: "rails/tube/noise retained downstream"
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Agent prompt structure}\label{app:prompt}

Each reflection round issues a single bounded JSON overlay. The model-facing
prompt is assembled from a fixed skeleton (role, isolation rules, current
state, GT-blind intrinsics, lean proxy features, allowed artifact images,
family parameter bounds, and the required output schema). Listing~\ref{lst:prompt-skeleton}
summarises the skeleton; Listing~\ref{lst:prompt-example} gives one example
proposal. GT mIoU and evaluation directories are never included.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Concentrated agent-prompt skeleton (per round).},label={lst:prompt-skeleton}]
Role: reflective parameter agent for SAM4Tun
Allowed context:
  - family reference priors / warm-start parameters
  - GT-blind intrinsics and lean proxy features
  - stage artifacts (depth map, detections, SAM masks)
  - ontology + bounded family search space
State: subset, family, stage-1 unlock?, anchor proxy, band
Task: diagnose failure mode; propose ONE overlay within bounds
Output JSON keys (required):
  observation, failure_mode, rationale, overlay
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Example JSON proposal returned by the agent.},label={lst:prompt-example}]
{
  "observation": "Centreline residual is high; unroll shows asymmetric voids.",
  "failure_mode": "f_curve_underfit (under-fit centre curve)",
  "rationale": "Raise polynomial degree and tighten RANSAC before retuning SAM.",
  "overlay": {
    "unfolding": {
      "polynomial_degree": 3,
      "ransac_threshold": 0.9
    }
  }
}
\end{lstlisting}
